4 Pricing examples

  1. Foreign-exchange model: two separately compounding currencies with volatile exchange rate.
    • Use (dollar cash bond) and (dollar price of pound) as Black-Scholes instruments to price claims.
    • Example 4.1 to price forward exchange rate contracts, and portfolio construction using terminal value pricing.

Foreign exchange

Definition 4.1 (Black-Scholes currency model) Consider the dollar cash bond \(B_t\), its sterling counterpart \(D_t\), and the dollar worth \(C_t\) of one pound. We assume \[ B_t = e^{rt}, \quad D_t = e^{ut} \] \[ C_t = C_0 \exp(\sigma W_t + \mu t) \]

How should we price a claim \(X\) denominated in pound units?

Intuition: the \(S_tD_t\), the dollar price of the pound bond, is a stock-like tradable.

Pricing proceeds by identifying the discounted pound process, zeroing the drift, and identifying \(C_t\) under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb Q\): \[\begin{align} Z_t &= B_t^{-1} D_t C_t \\ &= C_0 \exp \left( \sigma W_t + (\mu + u - r)t \right) \\ dZ_t &= Z_t \left[ \sigma\, dW_t + \left( \mu + u - r + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2} \right)\, dt \right] = Z_t \sigma d\tilde W_t \\ Z_t &= C_0 \exp \left( \sigma \tilde W_t - \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\, t \right) \end{align}\] Define the discounted claim \(E_t = \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}(B_T^{-1} X\mid \mathcal F_t)\) and use martingale representation to obtain \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dZ_t\).

Definition 4.2 (FX claim pricing) Claims in foreign currency have arbitrage value \[ V_t = B_t \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[B_T^{-1} X\mid \mathcal F_t] \] where \(\mathbb Q\) is the martingale measure for \(Z_t\).

Example 4.1 (forward FX contract) Consider the forward contract payoff \(X=C_t-k\): calculate \[ V_t = e^{-r(T-t)} \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[C_T - k\mid \mathcal F_t] \] Let’s first determine the arbitrage strike \(k\): recalling \(C_t = Z_t B_t D_t^{-1}\) yields (arbitrage price means that value of claim at expiry is \(0\)): \[\begin{align} k&=\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[C_T\mid \mathcal F_T] = C_0 \, \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} \exp\left[ \sigma \tilde W_T + \left( r - u - \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2} \right) T \right] = C_0 e^{(r-u)T} \end{align}\] Note that \(\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[C_T\mid \mathcal F_t] = e^{(r-u)(T-t)} C_t\) since \(C_t=Z_tB_tD_t^{-1}\) carries drift. Under this strike, the contract’s price at time \(t\) is \[\begin{align} V_t &= e^{-r(T-t)}\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[C_T - C_0 e^{(r-u)T} \mid \mathcal F_t] \\ &= \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[e^{rt - rT} e^{(r-u)(T-t)} C_t - C_0 e^{rt - uT} \mid \mathcal F_t] \\ &= e^{-uT}\left( e^{ut} C_t - e^{rt}C_0 \right) \end{align}\] To obtain the portfolio, calculate the discounted value and calculate \(dE_t / dZ_t = e^{-uT}\): this is the amount of pound sterling needed to replicate the claim. \[\begin{align} E_t &= B_t^{-1}V_t = e^{-uT}(e^{(u-r)t} C_t - C_0) = e^{-uT}(Z_t - Z_0) \end{align}\] The dollar cash position is \(E_t = \phi_t Z_t = -e^{-uT}C_0\).
