5 Interest rates

Bond basics

Definition 5.1 (discount bond) A discount bond is a promise to pay \(1\) dollar at time \(T\) for a discounted price \(P(0, T)\), so \(P(T, T)=1\). The market price is denoted \(P(t, T)\).

  1. Bond prices is a two-dimensional surface.
  2. The more distant the payment maturity, less current worth.
  3. Boundary condition \(P(\forall T, T)=1\).

Definition 5.2 (yield) Given current price \(P(t, T)\), its compounded price at \(T=t\) is (assuming constant compounding) \[ P(T, T) = 1 = e^{R(t, T)(T-t)}P(t, T)\implies R(t, T) = -\dfrac{\log P(t, T)}{T-t} \] The yield \(R(t, T)\) is the implied continuous compounding rate to justify the price of the bond beginning at \(t\) and maturing at \(T\). We can recover the price by \[ P(t, T) = \exp \left[ -(T-t)R(t, T) \right] \]

  1. Assuming stable interest rates, long-term yields are generally higher.
  2. If long-term rates are expected to fall, yield curve becomes inverted.

The instantaneous rate, or short rate, is the rate of borrowing money at the present at time \(t\). It is given by \[ r_1(t) = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0} R(t, t+\epsilon) = -\partial_{2} \log P(t, t) \]

The short rate entails the instantaneous rate of borrowing right now, but what about the projected instantaneous rate at some future time \(T\)?

Definition 5.3 (forward rate) The projected (at time \(t\)) rate of instantaneous borrowing at time \(T\) is \[ f(t, T) = -\partial_{T} \log P(t, T) \] To compute this, consider a forward contract which entails agreeing at time \(t\) to make a payment at \(T_1>t\) and receive payment at \(T_2\).

  • To replicate the ability to fulfill this contract at \(t\), we need to buy \(T_2\) bond and short some \(k\) units of \(T_1\) bond.
  • The current price of this portfolio is \(P(t, T_2) - kP(t, T_1)\).
  • Setting this to \(0\) yields the arbitrage price (at reference \(t\)) \(k=P(t, T_2)/P(t, T_1)\).
  • Then \(k=P_t(T_1, T_2)\) is the forward price of purchasing \(T_2\)-bond at \(T_1\), striked at time \(t\). The corresponding implied yield of this price is \[ R_t(T_1, T_2) = -\dfrac{\log k}{T_2-T_1} = -\dfrac{\log P(t, T_2) - \log P(t, T_1)}{T_2-T_1} \] Take \(T_2\to T_1\) to obtain the instantaneous rate. The bond price can be recovered by \[ P(t, T) = \exp \left( -\int_t^T f(t, u)\, du \right) \]

The yield curve \(T\mapsto R(t, T)\) measures the cumulative effect of forward rates (e.g. yield of \(3\)-year bond cumulatively accounts for rates at \(1, 2, 3\) years. The forward rate isolates this effect.

Simple rate model

Definition 5.4 (simple rate model) Given an integrable forward rate curve \(f(0, T)\), the forward curve evolves as \[\begin{align} d_t f(t, T) &= \sigma\, dW_t + \alpha(t, T)\, dt \\ f(t, T) &= \sigma W_t + f(0, T) + \int_0^t \alpha(s, T)\, ds \end{align}\] Here we ask \(\alpha\) to be a bounded, deterministic function.

  1. Fixing \(t\), forward rates at different maturities are deterministic \[ f(t, S) - f(t, T) = \int_0^t \alpha(s, S) - \alpha(s, T)\, ds \]
  2. Differences throughout time: all bonds share the same random source of movement \(\sigma W_{\delta t}\). \[\begin{align} f(t_1, S) - f(t_0, S) &= \sigma (W_{t_1} - W_{t_0}) + \int_{t_0}^{t_1} \alpha(s, S)\, dS \end{align}\]

Let’s consider two tradable securities:

  1. Numeraire: cash bond formed by the instantaneous rate \(r_t\): \[ dB_t = r_t B_t\, dt, \quad B_0 = 1 \] Noting that \(r_t = f(t, T)\) yields \(r_t = \sigma W_t + f(0, t) + \int_0^t \alpha(s, t)\, ds\). Substitute \[\begin{align} B_t &= \exp \left(\int_0^t r_s\, ds\right) = \exp \left( \sigma\, \int_0^t W_s\, ds + \int_0^t f(0, s)\, ds + \int_0^t \int_0^s \alpha(u, s)\, du \, ds \right) \\ &= \exp \left( \sigma\, \int_0^t W_s\, ds + \int_0^t f(0, s)\, ds + \int_0^t \int_s^t \alpha(s, u)\, du \, ds \right) \end{align}\] Note that the last two double-integrals are equivalent by \((0\leq u\leq s\leq t)\cong (\leq s\leq u\leq t)\).
  2. For the other tradable, pick bond at price \(T\) and compute \[\begin{align} P(t, T) &= \exp \left(-\int_t^T f(t, u)\, du\right) \\ &= \exp \left[-\int_t^T \left( \sigma W_t + f(0, u) + \int_0^t \alpha(s, u)\, ds \right)\, du\right] \\ &= \exp -\left[ \sigma (T-t) W_t + \int_t^T f(0, u)\, du + \int_0^t \int_t^T \alpha(s, u)\, du\, ds \right] \end{align}\]

Compute the discounted “stock” \(Z_t=B_t^{-1} P(t, T)\): \[\begin{align} -\log Z_t &= \sigma(T-t)W_t + \sigma\int_0^t W_s\, ds + \underbrace{\int_0^T f(0, s)\, ds}_{\text{constant}} + \int_0^t \int_s^T \alpha(s, u)\, du\, ds \\ d[(T-t)W_t] &= -W_t\, dt + (T-t)\, dW_t, \quad d\left[ \int_0^t W_s\, ds \right] = W_t\, dt \\ -\dfrac{dZ_t}{Z_t} &= \sigma(T-t)\, dW_t + \left[ \int_t^T \alpha(t, u)\, du - \dfrac{\sigma^2(T-t)^2}{2}\right]\, dt \end{align}\] Invoke CMG theorem to shift to a measure \(\mathbb Q\) such that \(\mathbb P\mapsto \mathbb Q\) effects \(dZ_t = -\sigma(T-t) Z_t\, d\tilde W_t\) by \[ d\tilde W_t = dW_t - \left[\dfrac{1}{\sigma(T-t)} \int_t^T \alpha(t, u)\, du - \dfrac{\sigma(T-t)}{2}\right]_{\gamma_t}\, dt \] Take any claim \(X\) expiring at time \(S\) and take the form the discounted claim process \(E_t = \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}(B_S^{-1}X\mid \mathcal F_t)\) and let \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dZ_t\).

Remark (replicating short-maturity bonds). Take \(X=1\) expiring at time \(S\) — this instrument corresponds to a \(S\)-bond. The claim above implies that every \(S\)-bond can be constructed from a \(T_{\geq S}\) bond and compounding cash. To prevent arbitrage, these prices must be the same. Let’s consider the implications:

  1. The discounted process \(Y_t=B_t^{-1}P(t, S)\) of the \(S\)-bond satisfies \[ -\dfrac{dY_t}{Y_t} = \sigma(S-t)\, dW_t + \left[ \int_t^S \alpha(t, u)\, du - \dfrac{\sigma^2(S-t)^2}{2}\right]\, dt \]

  2. Define the (term, time)-dependent drift function \[ (S, t)\mapsto \gamma_t^S = -\dfrac{\sigma(S-t)}{2} + \dfrac{1}{\sigma(S-t)} \int_t^S \alpha(t, u)\, du \] such that \(dY_t = -\sigma (S-t) Y_t(dW_t+\gamma_t^S\, dt)\).

  3. Equivalently using \(\mathbb Q\) for martingale-ing the \(T\)-bond, we have \[ dY_t = -\sigma(S-t)Y_t[d\tilde W_t + (\gamma_t^S - \gamma_t^T)\, dt] \] Recall that no-arbitrage implies that every tradable instrument must be driftless (martingale) under the risk-neutral measure, which means that \(\gamma_t^S = \gamma_t^T\). In particular, this implies that \(\partial_{T} \gamma_t^T = 0\).

  4. Use the definition for \(\gamma_t^T\) and differentiate upon multiplying both sides by \(\sigma(T-t)\) to obtain \[\begin{align} \sigma \gamma_t^T &= \partial_{T} \left[\sigma(T-t) \gamma_t^T\right] \\ &= \partial_{T} \left[ -\dfrac{\sigma^2(T-t)^2}{2} + \int_t^T \alpha(t, u)\, du \right] \\ &= -\sigma^2(T-t) + \alpha(t, T) \\ \alpha(t, T) &= \sigma^2(T-t) + \sigma \gamma_t \end{align}\] Two salient takeaways:

  5. The drift is constrained to be the sum of a maturity-independent term \(\sigma\gamma_t\) plus a particular function \(\sigma^2(T-t)\).

  6. The time-dependent \(\gamma_t\) is, again the market price of risk upon recognizing, for \(\Sigma = \sigma(T-t)\). \[ \dfrac{d_t P(t, T)}{P(t, T)} = -\Sigma(t, T)\, dW_t + (r_t - \Sigma(t, T) \gamma_t)\, dt \]
