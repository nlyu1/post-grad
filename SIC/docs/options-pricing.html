Preliminaries

Definition 3.1 (portfolio) A (stock-bond) portfolio is a pair \((\phi_t, \psi_t)\) of real-valued processes. The security component \(\phi\) should be \(\mathcal F\)-previsible.

A portfolio is self-financing if \[ dV_t = \phi_t\, dS_t + \psi_t\, dB_t \] Note that the value of the portfolio is \[ V_t = \phi_t\, S_t + \psi_t\, B_t \] The self-financing equation is non-trivial (trivial only when \(\phi, \psi\) are constant), else the time-derivatives of the product rules must cancel out.

Note that left-continuous (left-limit approaches correctly) \(\phi_t(\forall \omega)\) is previsible.

Example 3.1 (non-trivial self-financing strategy) Consider \((\phi_t = 2W_t, \psi_t = -t - W_t^2)\). Here we hold double the stock price’s amount of shares, and short \(t+W_t^2\) amount of cash. Calculating \(V_t = 2W_t^2 - W_t^2 - t = W_t^2 - t\) and checking the SDE: \[\begin{align} dV_t &= d(W_t^2 - t) = 2W_t\, dW_t \\ \phi_t\, dW_t + (\psi_t\, dB_t)_{=0} &= 2W_t\, dW_t \end{align}\]

Definition 3.2 (replicating strategy) A replicating strategy for a claim \(X\) which depends up to time \(T\) is a self-financing portfolio \((\phi, \psi)\) such that \(\|\sigma\phi\|_{T, 2} < \infty\) and \(V_t = \phi_tS_t + \psi_T B_t = X\).

Black-Scholes

Definition 3.3 (Black-Scholes model) Assuming deterministic \(r, \mu, \sigma\) such that the bond price \(B_t\) and stock price \(S_t\) follows \[ B_t = e^{rt}, \quad S_t = S_0 \exp(\sigma W_t + \mu t) \]

Zero rates

For simplicity, first assume that \(r=0\). The three steps to replication are:

  1. Find a risk-free measure \(\mathbb Q\) under which \(S_t\) is a martingale.
  2. Form the claim process \(E_t = \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[X|\mathcal F_t]\).
  3. Find a previsible process such that \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dS_t\). Then \(\phi_t\) is the stock holding.

For step 1, construct the SDE using Ito (definition 2.6): \[\begin{align} dS_t &= S_t \left[\sigma\, dW_t + \left(\mu + \dfrac {\sigma^2} 2\right)\, dt\right] \end{align}\] Next, kill the \((\mu + \sigma^2/2)/\sigma\) drift by shifting to a \(\mathbb Q\)-measure using CMG theorem 2.1 under which \((\mathbb P, W_t)\mapsto (\mathbb Q, \tilde W_t=W_t + (\mu/\sigma + \sigma/2)t)\) and \[ dS_t = \sigma S_t\, d \tilde W_t \]
Then \(S_t\) is a martingale under theorem 2.4.

For steps \(2\) and \(3\), form the martingale claim process \(E_t\) and invoke the martingale representation theorem to obtain \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dS_t\). Consider the portfolio \[ \left(\phi_t, \psi_t = E_t - \phi_t S_t \right) \] Note that for \(r=0\), we have \(B_t=1\) so \(V_t = \phi_t S_t + \psi_t B_t = E_t\), so \(dV_t = dE_t = \phi_t\, dS_t\) by construction in step \(3\), so the portfolio is self-financing.

  1. Under the exponential-Brownian assumption, \(\mathbb Q=\mathbb P\iff \mu = -\dfrac 1 2 \sigma^2\).
  2. Substituting \(\sigma \tilde W_t = \sigma W_t + (\mu + \sigma^2/2) t\), we find that \(S_t\) is tractable under the new measure: \[ S_t = S_0 \exp \left( \sigma \tilde W_t - \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2} t \right) \]

Non-zero rates

Consider constant, nonzero rates \(r\). Form a discounted stock \(Z_t = B_t^{-1} S_t\) and discounted claim \(B_T^{-1}X\). These values are discounted w.r.t. \(t=0\). Then \[\begin{align} Z_t &= S_0 \exp \left[ \sigma W_t + (\mu - r) t \right] \\ dZ_t &= Z_t \left[ \sigma\, dW_t + \left(\mu - r + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\, dt \right] \end{align}\] In short, \((S_t, \mu)\mapsto (Z_t, \mu - r)\). The new martingale measure \(\mathbb Q\) is formed analogously. Similarly construct \(E_t = \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[B_T^{-1}X \mid \mathcal F_t]\) and use the martingale representation theorem to form \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dZ_t\).

  • Let’s assess holding \(\phi_t, \psi_t=E_t - \phi_t Z_t\) (stock, bond) in the real world. Note that bond holding is calculated based on discounted stock \(Z_t\), not original stock.
  • Value process \(V_t = \phi_t S_t + (E_t - \phi_t B_t^{-1} S_t) B_t = E_t\, B_t \implies dV_t = B_t \, dE_t + E_t\, dB_t\)
    • Product rule follows from the zero-volatility assumption for the \(B_t\) process.
  • On the other hand, note that \(dS_t = d(B_tZ_t)\) and \(\phi_t\, dZ_t = dE_t\) to match: \[\begin{align} \phi_t\, dS_t + \psi_t\, dB_t &= \phi_t\, dS_t + E_t\, dB_t - \phi_t Z_t\, dB_t \\ &= \phi_t B_t\, dZ_t + \phi_t Z_t\, dB_t + E_t\, dB_t - \phi_t Z_t\, dB_t \\ &= B_t\, dE_t + E_t\, dB_t = dV_t \end{align}\]

Details of the discount process:

  • The martingale construction \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dZ_t\) says that changes in the discounted claim’s value should be equivalent to changes in the discounted stock’s value. Both are discounted.
  • From \(V_t=E_tB_t\), changes in the portfolio price (which is the undiscounted \(\mathbb Q\)-value of the claim measured at time \(t\) is due to two pieces:
    • \(B_t\, dE_t\) (change in discounted value).
    • \(E_t\, dB_t\) (interest rate effect).
  • From a holdings perspective, changes in the stock price \(\phi_t\, dS_t\) contributes a \(B_t\, dE_t\) term (desired) and a \(\phi_t Z_t\, dB_t\) term which counteracts the relevant term in cash holding value difference.

Theorem 3.1 (Black-Scholes pricing) Under Black-Scholes assumptions for prices and interest rates, all integrable claims \(X\) at horizon \(T\) have replicating strategies with arbitrage price (at time \(t\)): \[ V_t = B_t \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[B_T^{-1}X \mid \mathcal F_t] = e^{-r(T-t)} \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[X\mid \mathcal F_t] \] where \(\mathbb Q\) is the martingale measure for the discounted stock \(B_t^{-1}S_t\).

Proposition 3.1 (European call) Consider the call option payout \(X(S_T) = (S_T-k)^+\). \[ V = e^{-rT} \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} (S_T-k)^+ \]

Proceeding to calculation, recall that \[\begin{align} d Z_t &= Z_t \left[ \sigma\, dW_t + \left(\mu - r + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\, dt \right] = \sigma Z_t \, d\tilde W_t \\ \sigma\, dW_t &= \sigma\, d\tilde W_t - \left(\mu - r + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\, dt \\ d\log S_t &= \sigma\, dW_t + \mu\, dt = \sigma\, d\tilde W_t + \left(r - \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\, dt \end{align}\] Under the martingale measure \(\mathbb Q\), \(\log S_t\sim \mathcal N((r-\sigma^2/2)t, \sigma^2)\).

  • The greater the volatility, the lower the drift.

Fix horizon \(T\) and introduce \(Z\sim \mathcal N(-\sigma^2t/2, \sigma^2)\), we have \[\begin{align} V &= e^{-rT} \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} (S_T-k)^+ \\ &= e^{-rT} \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} (S_0 e^{Z+rT}-k)^+ = \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} (S_0 e^{Z}-k e^{-rT})^+ \end{align}\]

Final words

Remark. To price an instrument with exercising optionality: suppose the claim purchaser has a set of options \(A\) and receives payoff \(X_a\) at time \(T(a)\), the issuer should charge \[ V = \sup_{a\in A} \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[B_{T(a)}^{-1} X_a] \]

Although the martingale representation theorem does not explicitly provide \(\phi_t\), we can use \(dE_t = \phi_t\, dS_t\implies \phi_t = \partial_{S} E {\big|}_t\).

Proposition 3.2 (terminal value pricing) Let \(X=f(S_T)\). Denote the value of derivative at time \(t\) by \[ V_t(s_t)=V(S_t, t) = B_t \,\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} \left[ B_T^{-1} f(S_T) \mid S_t =s_t \right] \] The trading strategy is given by \(\phi_t = \partial_{S_t} V_t {\big|}_{s_t, t}\). This amount of stock holdings correctly equates the change in replicating portfolio price relative to stock price change

Recall that \(\sigma\, dW_t = \sigma\, d\tilde W_t + \dots\), then \(S_t = e^{\sigma W_t + \mu t}\) implies the SDE form \[ dS_t = S_t \left[ \sigma \, dW_t + \left(\mu + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right) t \right] = S_t (\sigma\, d\tilde W_t + r\, dt) \] Directly applying Ito \(V(S_t, t)\) yields (here derivatives are taken w.r.t. \(S_t=s_t\)) the SDE: \[\begin{align} dV_t &= \left( \sigma S_t\, \partial_{S} V \right)\, d\tilde W_t + \left( \dfrac{\sigma^2 S_t^2}{2} \partial_S^2 V + r S_t\, \partial_{S} V + \partial_{t} V \right)\, dt \end{align}\] On the other hand, using the self-financing condition yields \[\begin{align} dV_t &= \phi_t\, dS_t + \psi_t\, (dB_t)_{=rB_t\, dt} \\ &= (\sigma S_t \phi_t)\, d\tilde W_t + (rS_t\phi_t + r\psi_tB_t)\, dt \\ &= (\sigma S_t \phi_t)\, d\tilde W_t + rV_t\, dt \end{align}\]

  1. Matching the volatility terms yield \(\phi_t = \partial_{S} V\).
  2. Matching the drift terms yield \[ \dfrac{\sigma^2 S_t^2}{2} \partial_S^2 V + rS_t \, \partial_{S} V - r V + \partial_{t} V = 0 \] Coupling with the boundary condition \(V(s, T)=f(s)\) yields the pricing equation.

Remark (miscellany). If an option is out of the money, both bond and stock holdings go to zerl. When option approaches expiration in-the-money, stock holding goes to \(1\) and bond to \(-k\).
